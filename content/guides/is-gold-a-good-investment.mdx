This is the question under all the others, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a sales pitch. **Gold is a good diversifier and a long-term store of value, but a poor income investment — it pays nothing, and it can go years without rising. Whether it's "good" depends on what you want it to do.** Used as ballast in a broader portfolio, it earns its place. Treated as a get-rich bet, it usually disappoints. Here's the honest picture.

## What gold does well

Gold's strengths are real and specific. **It preserves purchasing power over long periods, it tends to hold up when stocks fall, and it carries no default risk because no one has to pay you back.**

- **A long-term store of value.** Over decades, gold has broadly kept pace with the cost of living. It's "money that can't be printed," which is why it holds value when currencies weaken.
- **Diversification.** Gold often moves differently from stocks, so a small slice can steady a portfolio when equities drop. That low correlation is its single most useful trait.
- **No counterparty risk.** A stock can go to zero and a bond issuer can default; physical gold is just gold. It doesn't depend on any company or government staying solvent.
- **A crisis hedge.** In wars, banking scares, and panics, money often flows into gold — useful insurance, even if the spikes fade.

## Where gold falls short

The weaknesses are just as real, and the ads never mention them. **Gold produces no income, can stagnate for years, costs money to hold, and isn't the short-term crisis lottery ticket it's often sold as.**

- **No income.** Gold pays no dividend or interest. A share of stock or a bond works for you; gold just sits there. Over long stretches, that lost income is a genuine cost.
- **Long flat (and down) periods.** Gold fell for most of the 1980s and 1990s and gave back large gains after 2011. "Gold only goes up" is a myth.
- **Holding costs.** Physical gold costs something to store and insure, and you pay a premium to buy and a spread to sell — friction that stocks bought cheaply don't carry.
- **It's volatile too.** Gold can drop sharply, including in the first days of a market panic when investors sell everything to raise cash.

<Callout label="Key takeaway">
Gold is **insurance and ballast, not an engine of growth.** A portfolio that's all gold gives up the long-run returns of productive assets; a portfolio with a small gold slice gets diversification without sacrificing much. The role you assign it is everything.
</Callout>

## Gold vs. stocks and bonds

The clearest way to judge gold is against the alternatives. **Stocks grow with the economy and pay dividends; bonds pay interest; gold does neither but behaves differently from both, which is exactly why it diversifies.**

<Figure caption="How gold stacks up against the assets people weigh it against.">

| | Gold | Stocks | Bonds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Income | None | Dividends | Interest |
| Long-run growth | Tracks inflation | Highest historically | Modest |
| Behavior in a crash | Often holds/rises | Falls | Mixed |
| Main risk | Long flat periods | Volatility, drawdowns | Inflation, rates |
| Role | Diversifier / insurance | Growth engine | Income / stability |

</Figure>

Notice the table doesn't crown a winner. Each asset does a different job. Gold's job is to behave unlike the others when it matters most — which is valuable precisely because it gives up income to do it. ## How much gold makes sense

There's no universal number, and anyone who states one without knowing your finances is guessing. **A commonly discussed range is 5%–10% of a portfolio in gold** — enough to diversify, not so much that you forfeit the growth of productive assets. Where you land depends on your age, your other holdings, and your tolerance for swings. It's a personal decision, ideally made with a professional who can see your whole situation.

## Who gold suits — and who it doesn't

- **It suits** someone who wants diversification and a hedge, holds a long time horizon, and treats gold as one slice of a broader mix.
- **It's a poor fit** for someone who needs income from the holding, expects steady year-by-year growth, or is hoping to time a quick profit.

The honest summary: gold is a good investment for the right *role*, in the right *size*, held for the right *reasons*. It's a bad investment for someone expecting it to do a job it was never built for.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is gold a good investment right now?**
That depends less on timing than on role. As a small, long-held diversifier, gold can make sense in many environments; as a short-term bet on the price, it's unpredictable. Decide why you want it before you worry about when to buy.

**Does gold beat the stock market?**
Over very long periods, stocks have generally produced higher total returns, partly because they pay dividends and grow with the economy. Gold's value is diversification and crisis protection, not beating stocks over decades.

**Why doesn't everyone just buy gold?**
Because gold pays no income and can stagnate for years, while stocks and bonds put your money to work. Most investors hold only a small slice of gold for diversification rather than going all in.

**Is gold safer than stocks?**
It's different, not strictly safer. Gold often holds up when stocks fall, but it has its own sharp swings and long flat stretches. Its value is behaving differently from stocks, which steadies a mixed portfolio.
