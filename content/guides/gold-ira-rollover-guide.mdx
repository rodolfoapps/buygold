This is the question people near retirement ask most, and it's where the worst sales pressure lives. So let's keep it plain. **A gold IRA rollover moves money from an existing retirement account — a 401(k) or traditional IRA — into a self-directed IRA that holds approved physical gold, without you taking the cash and triggering taxes.** Done as a direct transfer, it's a routine, tax-free move. Done carelessly, it can cost you in taxes and penalties.

## What a gold IRA actually is

A gold IRA is a **self-directed IRA** — an IRA that's allowed to hold alternative assets, including physical precious metals, instead of just stocks and funds. It has the same contribution limits and tax treatment as a regular IRA. The differences are practical:

- A **custodian** administers the account (a regular IRA has one too, but here it must be one that handles metals).
- A **depository** stores the physical metal in an approved, insured vault. **You cannot keep IRA gold at home** — doing so is treated as a withdrawal.
- You buy approved metal through a dealer, and the custodian settles it into your account.

## Direct vs. indirect rollover — get this right

There are two ways to move the money, and the difference matters more than anything else on this page.

<Figure caption="Direct vs. indirect rollovers. When in doubt, choose direct.">

| | Direct transfer | Indirect (60-day) rollover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How money moves | Custodian to custodian; you never touch it | Check comes to you; you redeposit it |
| Tax/penalty risk | Very low | High if you miss the deadline |
| Deadline | None | 60 days to redeposit |
| Withholding | None | Plan may withhold 20% |
| Frequency limit | Unlimited | One indirect IRA rollover per 12 months |

</Figure>

A **direct transfer** (sometimes called a trustee-to-trustee transfer) sends the funds straight from your old account to the new custodian. You never receive the money, so there's nothing to tax. **This is the route to use in almost every case.**

An **indirect rollover** sends *you* a check, and you have **60 days** to deposit the full amount into the new account. Miss the window and the IRS treats it as a distribution — income tax, plus a 10% early-withdrawal penalty if you're under 59½. Worse, your old plan may withhold 20% up front, which you then have to make up from your own pocket to roll over the full amount. The traps are real and avoidable: choose direct.

<Callout label="The one mistake to avoid">
Don't take the check yourself unless you have a specific reason. A **direct, custodian-to-custodian transfer** sidesteps the 60-day rule, the 20% withholding, and the once-per-year limit in one move.
</Callout>

## Which metals are allowed

The IRS sets purity standards. In broad terms, IRA gold must be **at least 99.5% pure**, and the metal must be an approved coin or bar from an accredited source. Common eligible products include American Gold Eagles (a specific exception) and many standard bullion coins and bars. Collectible and "rare" coins are generally **not** allowed — which is notable, because over-marked collectible coins are exactly what high-pressure sellers push. Your custodian can confirm any specific product before you buy.

## The fees nobody mentions up front

A gold IRA costs more to run than a plain index-fund IRA. None of these are disqualifying — but you should see them clearly before you start:

- **Account setup fee** — one-time, often around \$50–\$100.
- **Annual custodian fee** — administration, commonly \$75–\$300 a year.
- **Annual storage fee** — the depository vault, often a flat fee or a small percentage of holdings.
- **The dealer premium** — the markup on the metal itself, the same premium discussed in [how to buy gold](/guides/how-to-buy-gold).

Ask for the full fee schedule in writing. A straight answer is a good sign; a runaround is not.

## The rollover, step by step

1. **Open a self-directed IRA** with a custodian that handles precious metals.
2. **Request a direct transfer** from your 401(k) or existing IRA to the new account. Your new custodian usually helps initiate this.
3. **Choose approved metal** with a reputable dealer and confirm eligibility with the custodian.
4. **Settle and store.** The custodian pays the dealer and the metal goes to the approved depository in your account's name.
5. **Keep your paperwork.** Confirmations and statements matter at tax time and when you eventually sell or take distributions.

## Is it right for you?

That's genuinely not a question a website can answer. A gold IRA suits some people who want a slice of hard assets inside tax-advantaged retirement money — and it's overkill or a poor fit for others. The fees, the loss of simplicity, and the [forces that move gold's price](/guides/what-moves-the-price-of-gold) all factor in. Talk it through with a tax or financial professional who can see your whole situation before you move a dollar.
